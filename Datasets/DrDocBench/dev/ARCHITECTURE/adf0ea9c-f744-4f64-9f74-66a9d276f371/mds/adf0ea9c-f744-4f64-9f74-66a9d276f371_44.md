The name 'Pashima' and the surface on which it is written compete in the same space for reference to Pashmina, as if, in the matter of reference, the plan was over-determined. Although we can only access the space of the word through the graphic space of the plan, they are different. This linguistic space has to do with the network of relations that 'Pashmina' has to the other names in the plan, to the place names of Manhattan, and to the associations that New Yorkers bring to these made-up names ('Pashmina' =/$\neq$ 'Pashtuns'). In this plan, signifiers shift inexplicably in relation to signifieds. This is one of the hallmarks of the foreign. For instance, Gaymenistan and the Lesbikhs are contiguous on the basis of two different types of association. The Gaymeni and Lesbikhs both hang out on the Lower West Side. Both groups are associated by their gender preferences. But on the other side of Central Parkistan, the Blahniks and the Bulimikhs are contiguous; this time the contiguity is based on alliteration.$^{11}$ In the former, the contiguity has to do with the signifieds, in the latter, with the signifiers. There is no apparent reason why it should be one way on the Hudson River and another on the East River. That's just the way it is with foreign lands and their customs. The successful tourist realises this, and comes back to his/her own culture enriched by the knowledge that it is equally conventional.$^{12}$





 The adjacencies and separateness of, for example, 'Pashmina' or 'Blahniks'
constitute a topological space that underlies but is not commensurate with the metric
space of Pashmina or the Blahniks. Without a rigorous demonstration, we would like to
claim for language the space of topology. Instead of the familiar properties (dimension,
angle, shape, proportion) of architectural space in which every name is confined to its
own room, topological space is organised into regions and their boundaries, in relations
of inclusion, intersection and foreign-ness. 'Pashmina' is not 3 m from 'Badassin'; it is
separated from 'Badassi' by 'Khlintunisia'. We are familiar with the spatiality of words
by the dream work which links latent to manifest content. (As Freud demonstrated in his
walking talking tour of Signorelli/Herzegovina, names impress themselves with greater
and lesser tenacity upon consciousness, and their strange allegiances come encumbered
with sex and death.) $^{13}$ Words are separated by displacement and joined by condensation
